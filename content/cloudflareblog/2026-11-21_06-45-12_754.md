Welcome to the 26th edition of Cloudflare's DDoS Threat Report. This edition covers the third quarter of 2026 — July, August, and September — and returns the series to its quarterly cadence following the combined H1 2026 volume. The analysis is produced by [Cloudforce One](https://www.cloudflare.com/cloudforce-one/), Cloudflare's Threat Intelligence organization, providing a comprehensive analysis of the evolving threat landscape of [Distributed Denial of Service (DDoS) attacks](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/what-is-a-ddos-attack/) based on data from the [Cloudflare network](https://www.cloudflare.com/network/).

## Key insights

1.  Short-burst, low-bandwidth attacks lasting under 10 minutes and 1 Gbps surged by 208%. Conversely, hyper-volumetric attacks exceeding 1 Tbps plummeted by nearly 69% from Q2, possibly a reflection of law enforcement actions against four major Internet of Things (IoT) botnets earlier in the year. However, variants continue to emerge, proving that IoT botnets remain a durable, high-volume threat to network infrastructure.
2.  Threat actors dramatically shifted their reflection and amplification tactics, abruptly abandoning CLDAP floods, which collapsed by 99.5% quarter-over-quarter. Instead, attackers pivoted heavily toward other connectionless protocols, driving a 215% surge in DNS amplification and a 122% increase in general UDP floods.
3.  Attack targets broadened significantly across both industries and geographies, with the United States reclaiming the most-attacked position from China. Canada and Israel experienced sharp spikes in targeted attacks — jumping 10 and 7 spots respectively — while juggling geopolitical tensions, trade disputes, and contentious domestic events. At the same time, the operational focus shifted away from media organizations toward a wider array of industry targets, including consumer services, Internet infrastructure, and adult entertainment.

## Q3 by the numbers: 8,200 DDoS attacks every hour

Across its network in the third quarter of 2026, Cloudflare autonomously mitigated 18.2 million network-layer DDoS attacks and 14.4 trillion HTTP DDoS requests. That works out to approximately 8,226 network-layer DDoS attacks per hour, or about 197,400 per day — one attack every 0.44 seconds. The quarter's mitigated traffic totaled 272.6 PB at the network layer and 95.1 PB at the HTTP layer.

## More swarms, fewer giants

Short-burst, mid-range, high-count attacks — the operational signature of DDoS-for-hire "booter" services — accelerated this quarter. These attacks primarily targeted computer software, gambling and casinos, and information technology industries. Cloudflare autonomously mitigated 18.2 million network-layer DDoS attacks in Q3 2026, a +208% increase from [the same quarter in 2025](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ddos-threat-report-2025-q3/):

*   96% ended in under 10 minutes
*   98% stayed under 1 Gbps, more than enough to overwhelm a server or website

On the other hand, network-level hyper-volumetric attacks (those exceeding 1 Tbps) reversed course, dropping to 251 attacks in Q3 from [805 in Q2](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ddos-threat-report-2026-h1/#hyper-volumetric-attacks-see-a-more-than-6x-surge) — coinciding with [law enforcement action](https://www.justice.gov/usao-ak/pr/authorities-disrupt-worlds-largest-iot-ddos-botnets-responsible-record-breaking-attacks) in early 2026 that dismantled four of the largest IoT DDoS botnets: Aisuru, KimWolf, JackSkid, and Mossad.

Still, IoT-based botnets remain a durable high-volume threat as the ecosystem churns through new variants such as [Dysphoria](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-dysphoria-ddos-botnet-spreads-to-200k-devices-worldwide/) and [Evooo1Bot](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/new-evooo1bot-linux-botnet-turns-routers-into-traffic-relay-nodes/). [Mirai](https://blog.cloudflare.com/inside-mirai-the-infamous-iot-botnet-a-retrospective-analysis/)\-classified mitigation peaked in August at 57.23 PB, representing 46.5% of all mitigated network-layer bytes in Q3, including four days — August 16, 19, 23, and 25 — that each cleared three petabytes. The quarter's longest sustained Mirai attack ran 49.7 hours between August 19 and August 21.

## Vector shifts: the collapse of CLDAP and rise of UDP

The most dramatic vector shift of the quarter was downward. [CLDAP](https://blog.cloudflare.com/reflections-on-reflections/) (Connectionless Lightweight Directory Access Protocol) attacks — where small queries with a forged victim IP address trick public domain controllers (UDP 389) into reflecting massive, amplified responses back at the target — experienced a dramatic +580% quarter-over-quarter surge in the first half of the year before collapsing to just 0.05% of attacks in Q3.

Meanwhile, [DNS amplification](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/dns-amplification-ddos-attack/) surged 215% to account for 3.43% of all network-layer attacks, exploiting open [DNS resolvers](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/dns/dns-server-types/) in a similar reflection-and-amplification pattern to CLDAP: small spoofed queries, large replies directed at the victim. Other [UDP floods](https://www.cloudflare.com/learning/ddos/udp-flood-ddos-attack/) climbed from 14.06% to 31.31% of network-layer attacks. UDP is a connectionless transport protocol, which makes it a convenient vehicle for raw volumetric attacks: attackers send large numbers of UDP packets to random or specific ports on the target, forcing the target to check for a listening application and respond, consuming its network and compute resources.

## Most-attacked industry

While Media, Production & Publishing remained the most-attacked industry in Q3 2026, its overall share is roughly a quarter of its Q2 level, and the focus has moved across a wider set of industries. Internet rose from outside the top 10 to #6; Consumer Services and Adult Entertainment also climbed in, reaching #8 and #9 respectively.

## Most-attacked locations

### Geographic surges: Canada and Israel climb the ranks

Adding to an already turbulent period of bilateral trade disputes, Canada jumped from #16 to #6, driven by attacks against its Retail, Telecommunications, Service providers & carriers, and Marketing & advertising industries.

Israel moved 7 spots to take #10 as a result of sustained DDoS activity in mid-September. While this period was marked by [contentious decisions](https://www.timesofisrael.com/election-panel-votes-to-bar-balad-chief-abu-shehadeh-over-alleged-support-for-terror/) ahead of parliamentary elections and the Rosh Hashanah holiday, there was also a massive, documented [surge in hostile cyber warfare](https://www.timesofisrael.com/iranian-cyberattacks-on-israel-surged-in-2026-cyber-chief-says/) targeting Israel this year.

### Top 10

The United States was the most-attacked location in Q3 2026, while China was number #2 and Indonesia rose into the top three at 2.93% of total attacks. The U.S.-China pairing at the top is consistent with the first half of 2026, when China finished #1 and the U.S. #2; the countries have traded positions as attention and attacker preferences shift.
