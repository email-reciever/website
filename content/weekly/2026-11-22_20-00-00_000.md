The Main Thing The Solid core team manifesting the 2.0 release Solid 2.0 is thriving Not much changes between a release candidate and a major, but Solid 2.0 came out this week and post-Cursor breakup, Solid appears to be entering their “30, flirty and thriving” era as a framework. If you’re curious about the new async features, we covered that in 513 when we wrote about the RC. But since then, after 23 more iterations, Ryan and co have finally put the finishing touches on this version of the framework. As a licensed JavaScript therapist, here’s how I can tell they are ready for their next serious relationship. Letting go of the past: One thing that went under the radar in the RC was Solid sunsetting their meta-framework SolidStart. Now server functions live in the framework core while the dev server, SSR and builds are handled by Solid’s Vite plugin, making integrations (like Clerk and Sentry) much easier to build and maintain. They also removed a bunch of redundant APIs and reduced the surface area of the framework. Nothing says healthy like letting go of the past. Embracing open communication: Solid’s development builds report errors as they happen with the location of the error, the recommended fix, and point your agent to skills it can use to do better in the future. The bigger theme is that observability for agents is now a first-class citizen of the framework. That open communication is the key to a good relationship. Ready to experiment: There are two bigger bets that the Solid team is working on. The first is ReactiveScript , a new language that compiles to Solid and turns its runtime warnings into compiler errors. The second is Reactive Server Components, which are coming in the next few weeks and send HTML diffs instead of JSON. No one wants someone who’s stuck in their ways so Solid’s willingness to try new things is “giving green flag”. Bottom line: Moving on after a breakup is hard, but with Solid 2.0’s release, they’re ready to get back into your app and experience love again.
