# How I build UIs

November 16, 2026

In the last two years of working as a staff front-end developer, I’ve unintentionally assimilated more and more design responsibilities.

It happened as a side effect.

Code got easier to write, so I finally had the time to spend on polish. The LLMs charge the same for slop and good UIs, so I thought I might as well pick the latter.

When I help out engineers from other disciplines _(back-end, infra, data)_, a question they always have is how I manage to figure out what to put on the screen, what color it should have, and how it should behave.

I tell them that I’m just a visual person _(because I want to keep my job)_.

But with the risk of spilling the sauce, this is the system I follow.

## I start with data

To everyone’s surprise, my first reflex isn’t to write a prompt or call an agentic skill.

LLMs are too convincing and they bias me.

Every software shows data and manipulates data. CQRS, for example, is a design principle which represents a system as a combination of queries (fetching data) and commands (changing data). GraphQL is designed around the same idea.

If a UI is an interface to the underlying system then it too, on an abstract level, has these capabilities.

So instead of thinking of components, I think about the data.

What do we want to show?

Can you get all that data?11 If it’s sitting in Snowflake and we can’t get it in a reasonable way, we’d need to solve that first.

1 If it’s sitting in Snowflake and we can’t get it in a reasonable way, we’d need to solve that first.

How do we want people to work with that data?

Let’s look at an example:

We want to show our support agents (the people, not the AI agents) everything about a merchant and give them the ability to suspend them.

What’s _everything_? What data is relevant to this decision? Is there an existing UI where they can see this data? Can we contribute to it instead of duplicating? Where does merchant data live? Is there an API for it that matches how support agents search for merchants?

How do suspensions happen currently? What forms can it take? Again, is there an API for it?

The UI is the tip of the icebergA small iceberg tip shaped like an interface sits above the waterline. Most of the iceberg sits below it, through three deepening layers of water, next to a squid.

This has nothing to do with the actual UI and everything to do with the product itself. If the product you’re trying to build is clear, the UI builds itself.

There are two ways this example can unfold.

We get positive and simple answers everywhere and we build them exactly what they’re asking for.

Or it turns out that they have entirely different needs and we should be thinking about an alerting system based on transactions instead of merchants.

The UI for these two things would be completely different.

## I look for ideas

As I’m thinking through the data and the actions, they slowly start turning into abstract shapes in my head.

Sketchbook page of UI thumbnailsA grid of twelve rough wireframe thumbnails, two crossed out, one circled, one left unfinished.

I draw on paper just to anchor my imagination to something.

Sketches I find to be very useful because they’re faster than any other tool22 Not only faster than LLMs, but the hand-to-eye connection helps me internalize and understand.. Even if I have what I consider to be a good idea, I force myself to sketch a few others, just for the thought exercise.

2 Not only faster than LLMs, but the hand-to-eye connection helps me internalize and understand.

I play around with the idea of having multiple pages, one page with drawers, dropdowns or multiple buttons.

When I feel like I’m out of my own ideas, I go look for inspiration on design websites, on X, in the products that I use myself. I like to think of this process as a funnel and I want to expand it as widely as possible.

Branching exploration with dead endsA single line branches repeatedly; most branches end in small crosses or trail off, and only two reach finished screens.

Then and only then, do I go to an LLM, I throw it a JSON without direction and see what it renders. Not because I’m looking for something fleshed out, but because I’m looking for more ideas and the same way I don’t want the LLM to bias me, I don’t want to bias it.

Maybe there’s something in its weights that I haven’t thought about.

This gives me an abstract idea of what I want to create.

It’s still shapes, and dots, and lines, but now I can start putting them together on the screen like Tetris blocks.

## I prototype

The mistake engineers make with UIs is that they hyperfocus on one design.

I think this comes from the idea of correctness.

There’s usually a _correct_33 At least from a financial point of view. way for data to be stored and manipulated to match performance, latency, and throughput targets.

3 At least from a financial point of view.

Design requires less accuracy.

That’s why there’s the notion that it’s easy.

There are many ways to solve a problem and being able to fit the data on the screen doesn’t necessarily mean that you’ve found the best solution.

Prototype in the actual codebase you will ship. Have your agent set up a prototype page where it can copy relevant bits of the existing code so you can iterate in isolation. Use a new branch for this, and deploy it to a temporary environment.

All that setup required time and discipline before, but now it’s so easy.

Tell your agent to prepare you three distinct prototypes in three different directions with the libraries you have available. Give it directions based on your research and the things you liked. Ask it to set them up with tabs or one under the other so you can easily swap between them. Pass it libraries, and screenshots.

I use [Emil Kowalski’s prototype skill](https://www.skills.sh/emilkowalski/skills/prototype) as a shortcut here.

Use a debug tool like Dialkit to wire up the relevant elements on the screen and play with their appearance. Ship this prototype page to your teammates so they can play with them too.

Play breeds ideas and prototyping should be play.

## I don’t use design tools

I never learned to work with design tools like Figma44 Beyond copying from them..

4 Beyond copying from them.

They’re probably still valuable to designers, but to me, they’re extra bookkeeping that I don’t need. I prefer to work straight in the environment that I’d be shipping and users will experience.

While moving things on the screen is more convenient with the mouse than with a prompt, I feel more comfortable in the code.

## I make a call

At some point you will have make a call.

This doesn’t have to be your own decision, though. The way I understand this part of the process is that I should try to guide the team through my taste, understanding of usability, and past experience.

But at the end of the day you’re building something for others.

I try to remember that:

*   Utility comes before beauty
*   What people like using is more important than what you like seeing

Ideas converging into one interfaceThree blurred versions of an interface drift toward the same spot. Where they meet they sharpen into one clear interface, then drift apart and blur again.

If I have to make a call myself, it depends on how pressed by time I am.

*   If I need to meet a deadline, I’ll pick something based on what matches my taste the best, and just go wth it. Having something good in prod is better than something perfect in my imagination.
*   If I have time on my hands, I’ll sleep on it. This sounds like abstract artist voodoo, but it works. There’s something going on in the subconcious. The context of research clutters the mind too much and a good night’s sleep refreshes the biological session.

I don’t drag this for too long, though.

If I can’t find a design I love in a day or two it means that my skills are not matching my taste and I just need to use this as a stepping stone.

This part requires gut feeling, intuition, and luck.

If it sounds too magical, remember that our profession relies on coding agents which are borderline sorcery. I think you can accept this.

## I get feedback

I never get feedback if I send someone an URL and ask them to tell me what they think.

Not good feedback, at least.

Silence means you’ve done a good job. Good feedback is unsolicited, accidental, often expressed through frustration.

Give people a ready tool and tell them to use it.

Corporations love demo sessions, but people either zone off or they can’t tell you anything by just looking at a UI. Real feedback comes from the problems they face and you usually get it in your DMs.

But only after they start actually using the thing.

## I iterate

Don’t change things pointlessly.

You’ve used products that have changed for the worse just for the sake of getting a redesign. Don’t do that.

That’s not iteration.

Put your ear close enough to the ground and you’ll find things to improve.

These are the true iterations.

The complaints, the small imperfections, the papercut bugs you put off, the bouncing elements, the flashing loading states.

Your skills will improve as you design so you will notice more and more of these.

## Random advice

*   I design the first version in black and white. I got this tip from [Refactoring UI](https://refactoringui.com/) back in the day, and I stick to it. Color should be frosting, not the main dish (especially in enterprise apps).
*   I prototype with [shadcn](https://ui.shadcn.com/). It has a great default black and white theme, and agents know the library well. I can swap it for something else later55 Like an internal component library.
    
    5 Like an internal component library
    

*   I don’t go overboard with animations. When I first learned about [motion](https://motion.dev/) (framer motion) I went a little too far. Animations are good for infrequent interactions. When people need speed, they need the UI to be snappy.
*   I use [Dialkit](https://www.dialkit.dev/) as a design debug tool.
*   I use SVGs for animated illustrations like the ones in this article.
*   Fashion and trends are a big part of making a UI. If you build a functional app with Bootstrap 3 today, people won’t like it even if it works perfectly.
*   I follow visual trends if I need to be in the mainstream. I focus on personality if I need to stand out.
