# Study the craft

August 3, 2026

![Dark Souls 3](/_astro/header.ChS0b1dF_2l9ULO.jpeg)

The best showcase for human skill is the Dark Souls streamers who can beat all 3 games back to back on a naked level 1 character armed with a club. No clothes, no upgrades, no tricks. Just a man and a dream.

Kind of goes the same for software.

I’m sure that no matter the agents I have access to, I won’t be able to produce a design as beautiful as [Josh Puckett](https://joshpuckett.me/).

I’ll make something good, no doubt about it. Through grit, careless permission approvals, and continuously renewing my usage limit, I’ll put something together. But someone better will make a more beautiful interface with nothing more than Sublime Text without a license and the Google search auto-generated AI response.

![](/_astro/image-2.3f6rwwYZ_avEbw.png)

A few days ago someone posted a game demo on X that Claude one-shotted.

![](/_astro/image-3.CD1PTCov_ZIDlzh.png)

People say you should let the agent take care of the details while you control the broad vision.

Brother, it’s all about the details.

Anyone equipped with the same off-the-shelf tools you have can build that flashy demo. But someone good at game dev can put some basic assets together with a better game loop, and people will prefer it. Access to tools is not a competitive advantage in an industry that still finds ways to open source its critical pieces.

But there’s a sense that if you’re not focusing on tools you’ll be left behind, stuck out of time.

You won’t.

Everybody’s behind. If you’re not working in a frontier lab right now and you get your news from a blog post, you’re already behind. Who knew about harness engineering and agentic coding 3 years ago? Who knows how we’ll be writing software in 3 more years? Only those who are pushing the technology forward have an idea. The rest of us are following.

There’s a notion that using the tool is a skill on its own.

But mastering a new tool like agentic coding is much easier for the skilled. Models get better with every generation. They will understand you more and more easily as time goes on, but you need to know what to ask of the genie.

We gravitate around patterns and ideas until something happens and turns the whole industry on its head.

The arguments about programming languages, frameworks, testing, and code quality we had before November 2022 have now turned into arguments about coding agents, models, harness setups, and tool usage.

Because no one knows the best way to build software.

Yet those who are more skilled consistently make better creations than those who put their trust in tools.
