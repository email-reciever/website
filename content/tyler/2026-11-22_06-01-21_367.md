## Cool Bits

1.  We wanted AI to cure cancer, but instead of Light The Night, we got [Patch the Planet](https://openai.com/index/coordinated-disclosure-of-critical-vulnerabilities/), an OpenAI initiative to disclose security vulnerabilities to package maintainers before they are announced to the public.
    
2.  [Sentry donated _one miliiiiiion dollars_ to open source maintainers this year](https://blog.sentry.io/another-year-another-1-000-000-to-open-source-maintainers/), which is objectively awesome. I’m glad some people still believe in open source 🥺.
    
3.  Built an app with Expo? [Enter it in the Expo App Awards](https://expo.dev/awards/2026) by October 31. Winners get a handcrafted metal cube, a prize you can’t explain to your family but will 100% keep on your desk. \[sponsored\]
    
4.  Speaking of paying open source maintainers, [The Open Source Pledge](https://opensourcepledge.com/) is an initiative to raise funds from companies to support important open source projects. Maybe tell your boss.
    
5.  The artist formerly known as Framer Motion [added `scopeMotion` and `useScopeMotion` in its 14.5 release](https://motion.dev/docs/scope-motion) for easy cleanup, media query matching, setting default transitions, and scoping to an element.
    
6.  [TanStack AI published its first stable release with version 1.0](https://tanstack.com/blog/tanstack-ai-1-0). Lots of stability happening right now in TanTown.
    
7.  [SerpApi’s new Extract API](https://serpapi.com/bytesdev-extract) lets your agents fetch a public web page and return the whole thing as structured JSON or as clean Markdown. Check out the [interactive demo](https://serpapi.com/bytesdev-extract). \[sponsored\]
    
8.  Colin McDonnell [built a library called Reylo](https://reylo.dev/) that lets you import `.sql` files into TypeScript with full type inference. I think this was the final nail in the coffin for the ORM startup era.
    
9.  Stencil Labs [open sourced `dquickts`](https://github.com/stencil-hq/dquickts), a fork of QuickJS that can natively run TypeScript with a durable execution engine. Can we get Michael Key to teach us all how to pronounce `dquickts`?
    
10.  [Greptile built the ultimate AI Code Review tool](https://www.greptile.com/?utm_source=bytes&utm_medium=sponsorship&utm_campaign=bytes_oct23) with AI agent swarms that review and test pull requests with full context of your codebase. \[sponsored\]
    
11.  [Kimi K3.1 Pro was just released](https://www.kimi.ai/blog/kimi-k3-1-pro) and it _appears_ to hold its own against Fable, Opus, and Astra on several benchmarks.
    
12.  Shu Ding wrote about [some of the ideas behind his GPU lexer](https://shud.in/thoughts/ideas-behind-gpu-lexer), which is a fancy (but slower than regex) way to do syntax highlighting in the browser. There’s also a Chinese proverb about why throwing bricks is good (unless you live in France, in which case maybe hold on to those bricks).
